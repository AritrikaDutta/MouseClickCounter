Section 0 — the problems this architecture exists to solveValidate these FIRST — every box below must answer to one of them
The market problem
INFR
Mid-tier miners face AISC pressure from declining head grade — a geology and mine-planning trend outside operational control, meaning every tonne processed yields less metal than it used to. The one lever fully within the operation's control is recovery: how much of the metal already present in the ore actually gets captured, rather than lost in the process. Recovery is process-driven and addressable in real time — regardless of what grade the mine plan hands the plant on any given day.

The data to optimize recovery already exists on-site: historian, dispatch, and lab systems that each do their own job well — but were never designed to talk to each other, feed decisions, or survive a cross-system audit. Meanwhile mandatory ESG and climate-disclosure regimes (ASIC in Australia, SEBI BRSR in India) now demand audit-grade data lineage these systems were never built to provide. The gap is not sensors or software — it is the architecture between them.

And even where data is unified, most mining AI deployments stall at the same point: recommendations sit in dashboards, and a busy operator must manually reconcile them against parts availability, dispatch schedules, and current plant state before acting — so adoption dies quietly. Closing that gap requires more than pipes — it requires a system that reasons across the connected data and proposes a decision-ready action, with a human still holding the final call.
The engineering problem — six requirements the architecture must satisfy
INFR
P1 · Fusion — combine OT sensor streams, lab assays, and mine-planning data into one decision-grade pipeline (different cadences, owners, and formats).
P2 · Sensor reality — survive real-world sensor failure (dropouts, stuck values, recalibration shifts) without silently poisoning models.
P3 · Parity — guarantee that features computed at training time are identical to features computed at inference time (no training-serving skew).
P4 · Last mile — deliver recommendations to operators inside their workflow, and capture their accept/override/reject response.
P5 · Traceability — make every reported metric traceable to its source readings, at audit/ESG grade.
P6 · Sustainability — run with a small team, at a platform cost a mid-tier operator will accept.
P7 · Agent scope — constrain what an agentic system may autonomously query versus act on; every action inside an explicit, auditable permission boundary, with the human gate (P4) as a hard stop before anything touches the physical plant.
every component below must trace to P1–P7 — if it solves none of them, it gets cut
